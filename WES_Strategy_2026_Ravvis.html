Overview
Executive Summary

Worldwide Evangelism Service (WES) is a Ghanaian youth-driven evangelism movement committed to spreading the gospel and documenting real outreach impact. Despite consistent branding and visible proof of ministry activity, WES is currently underperforming on social media — not because of a lack of content, but because of a distribution and storytelling gap.

This 6-month SOSTAC strategy (June – November 2026) is built around TikTok as the primary discovery engine, Instagram as the community growth platform, Facebook as the ministry hub, and Twitter/X for consistent visibility. The strategy transforms WES's real outreach activities into compelling short-form video content that grows a loyal audience, attracts volunteers, and invites support.

1.3 SWOT Analysis

✦ Strengths

  • Strong, clear mission and brand identity
  • Consistent visual branding across platforms
  • Real, documented outreach. Powerful raw material.
  • Active in the field. Constant content opportunities.
  • Existing Linktree with contact/activity access
  • Credible ministry track record

✦ Weaknesses

  • Inconsistent posting schedule (clustered, not spread)
  • No TikTok account. Missing the primary growth platform.
  • No video/Reels strategy on Instagram
  • Zero comment/engagement response culture
  • Twitter/X is private. Zero visibility.
  • No testimonies or social proof content

✦ Opportunities

  • TikTok is the #1 discovery platform for Gen Z faith content
  • 7.95M active social media users in Ghana and growing
  • WhatsApp community building for retention (91.8% of GH internet users)
  • Faith-based UGC and testimony storytelling is high-trust
  • Underserved niche: boots-on-ground outreach documentation
  • YouTube Shorts for long-term searchable evangelism content

✦ Threats

  • Competing ministries (PENSA, AOG Youth) with larger followings
  • Algorithm dependency. Declining organic reach for static posts.
  • Short attention spans. Short-form video dominance is non-negotiable.
  • Budget constraints limiting paid amplification
  • Irregular outreach schedules may disrupt content calendar
  • Volunteer burnout if content demands exceed team capacity

Closing Statement

WES doesn't have a content problem. They have a distribution and storytelling problem. The outreach is happening. The impact is real. The people are there. This strategy builds the system that captures that reality and turns it into consistent, compelling digital content that grows an audience, builds trust, and invites a new generation of Ghanaian Christians to stop watching and start moving.

TikTok first, because that is where discovery happens. Instagram second, because that is where community lives. Facebook to anchor the ministry. Twitter/X to stay visible. This platform priority order is right for where WES is right now. Do that consistently, and the growth will follow.
